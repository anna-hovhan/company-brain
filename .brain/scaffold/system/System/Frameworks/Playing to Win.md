# Playing to Win

## For future agent
This note explains the Playing to Win strategy choice cascade (A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin, 2013): five linked choices from winning aspiration to management systems, and how Company Brain records them and links them to OKRs, canvases, and the roadmap.

## What it is
Lafley and Martin define strategy as an integrated set of choices that positions a company to win (Playing to Win: How Strategy Really Works, Harvard Business Review Press, 2013). The choices cascade, each constraining the next:
1. **Winning aspiration**: the purpose of the enterprise and what winning looks like.
2. **Where to play**: the playing field: customer segments, geographies, offers, channels, value-chain stages. Also where not to play.
3. **How to win**: the way to create unique value there, through cost leadership or differentiation.
4. **Capabilities**: the activities and capabilities that must be in place to win where you chose to play.
5. **Management systems**: the systems and measures that support the choices.

## How to use it
1. Draft all five choices quickly, then iterate: a where-to-play choice that has no credible how-to-win is not a choice.
2. Make "where not to play" explicit; it is where most of the strategic value is.
3. Test coherence: every choice should reinforce the ones above and below it.
4. Ask "what would have to be true" for each choice to be a good one, and test the weakest condition first.
